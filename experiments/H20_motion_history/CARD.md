# H20 · The car's own motion over the last 4 s, read by the generator and the scorer, makes the driving better

**Siddesh** · AlpaSim, offline · ⏸ parked (negative) · 17 Nov – 20 Nov 2026 · hypothesis

[← all experiments](../../README.md#hypothesis-experiments) · [the account](README.md) · [experiment.toml](experiment.toml)

**Builds on:** [H17](../H17_generator_scorer_memory/CARD.md)

## Question

Drive-JEPA plans each moment from two camera frames 0.5 s apart and the car's current state. H05 and H17 found that the camera's past adds little, and in both the stronger signal was the car's own motion over the last few seconds. Does giving the generator and the scorer that motion, trained with them, make the model drive better in closed loop?

## Hypothesis

Eight past states of the car (the last 4 s, one every 0.5 s, built from poses only), encoded and added through two links that start at zero, one into the generator's plan starting points and one into the scorer's rating head, and trained with the generator and the scorer from Drive-JEPA 0.1.8b (pass 0.6) on Physical AI AV, raise the mean closed-loop score on 30 AlpaSim scenes. The rule written before training: it passes only if the model shown the history scores at least 0.8194 on average (the starting model's 0.7527 plus 0.0667, two scenes of 30) and above the same training with the history always hidden. Anything else refutes it.

## Predictions

⚠️ Not pre-registered: the rule was written on 17 Nov 05:01 and hashed (sha256 f8f4b975…a450, amendment 1 at 07:10) before the first training step on 18 Nov 03:09, in a folder outside git, so no commit date shows it

## What we learned

Giving the generator and the scorer the car's own motion over the last 4 s did not improve closed-loop driving. With the history shown, the mean over 30 AlpaSim scenes was 0.7771, against 0.7527 for the starting model and 0.8431 for the same training with the history always hidden. The rule written beforehand needed 0.8194 and a win over the hidden copy, and it failed both. Offline, both copies passed the pull-away check (0.755 and 0.764 against a floor of 0.66), yet in driving the copy shown the history stalled on a scene the starting model completes (d2715023: 1.2 m travelled against 44.4 m for the human driver, score 0.03), so a steady offline pull-away rate did not predict pull-away in the car. Training was not repeatable step for step until PyTorch's grid_sample backward, which uses atomic adds, was replaced by a deterministic one; the starting model's own training used the same non-repeatable path (argued from the code, not re-measured). The hidden copy's gain is an observation, not a tested result: it has no rule of its own. Limits: one seed, one checkpoint per copy, 30 scenes.

## Checks that decided something

- **What does the simulator's car state hold during a drive?** (17 Nov): The simulator reports zero speed and acceleration for the first 3 s of every drive (its warm-up) while the car's poses move, so a history read from the reported speed would show a car standing still. The history is therefore built from poses only. → build every history row from poses, the same way in training and in the car
- **The starting model's closed-loop baseline, and whether its drives repeat** (17 Nov): The starting model's windowed mean is 0.7527 on the 30 scenes (0.5919 on the 12 diagnosis scenes, 0.8599 on the other 18); its repeats on a rebuilt simulator image were bit-identical per tick. Its pull-away go rate on the 106 fixed moments is 0.840. → the pass line of the rule written beforehand: 0.7527 + 0.0667 = 0.8194
- **The history data: complete, and built the same way as in the car** (17 Nov): 4,015 files, one per training clip, 126,981 moments; 25.0 % of moments have fewer than 8 valid rows (3,971 have none). The four builder tests pass: no future leak, the relative pose matches both planners, interpolation matches the converter, shape and valid flag. → train on all moments, with the valid flag as the only signal of missing rows
- **At step 0 the new model is exactly the starting model** (18 Nov): On the 1,131 graded picking moments, both copies' picks and plans equal the starting model's on every moment and the rating logits differ by at most 0.0; the cached encoder tokens are bit-identical to the live encoder's. → train
- **Does training repeat bit for bit after a stop and resume?** (18 Nov): Not at first: the same step on the same state gave different gradients on 33 of 79 tensors, because PyTorch's CUDA backward of grid_sample uses atomic adds. With that backward replaced by a deterministic one (det_grid_sample.py), 6 steps straight and 3 + resume + 3 are identical in losses, all 79 weight tensors, the optimiser states and the random states. Re-run on CPU on 21 Nov from the kept files: still identical, and the kept pre-fix pair still differs. → train with the deterministic backward; resume after a crash is the same run
- **Does the rebuilt simulator image reproduce the starting model's earlier drives?** (20 Nov): Yes on all 8 scenes named beforehand: per-tick metric rows bit-identical to the recorded drives. For the 4 drives reused from H17's image check, that they ran on this image is inferred from timing, not logged. → drive both copies on this image (alpasim-base 0.134.0, id ab16bc78...; renderer nre-ga 26.04, 97f43e71...)
- **Does the new simulator driver reproduce the starting model at step 0?** (20 Nov): Yes: the step-0 checkpoint driven through the new driver on e3ed554f is per-tick bit-identical to the starting model's recorded drive and to its re-run on this image. → drive the two chosen checkpoints
