Swerve vocabulary, checked on real ticks

Drive-JEPA never proposes a plan that steers around an oncoming car, so neuroNCAP's frontal scenarios cap it at about what braking alone can score. This page proposes adding 32 extra swerve paths to its candidate set and training a scorer for them on cached features. Below, those 32 paths are drawn and graded on six real ticks from our reconstructed scenes, for you to check before anything is built.

What this says

The two data sources pull in opposite directions. In real logs a car ahead is nearly always a lead car going the same way, and a short braking plan behind it is safe; trained on real collision labels, the scorer learns exactly that, and on our frontal scenes it then rates the braking plan as safe (AUC 0.26 to 0.36, worse than chance). Trained on rendered collision labels only, it learns the frontal lesson, but that answer does not carry over to real camera images: on normal real driving it would override the released plan on half of all ticks. No weighting found a middle. Telling a lead car from an oncoming one needs the motion between the two frames, and nothing in the scorer's input makes that explicit.

  • Also measured: the released model's own auxiliary heads do not help (key-agent collision head AUC 0.52; area head 0.65 to 0.71, below ours). A hazard-localisation target, dropping frontal-0064, and a gate threshold chosen on the other folds did not fix frontal either.
  • The vendor model mixes the ticks in a batch: batch 1 reproduces what it drove, batch 4 drifts up to 1 m. The caches are now built at batch 1; earlier gallops training ran at batch 8.
  • Every tick, rendered and real, can be inspected in the target viewer on shakaal, port 8932.

First results: does it generalise? (9 Oct, overnight)

The cache is built: 450 episodes, 21,934 ticks, 64 plans each (32 native and 32 from the revised vocabulary), with every plan graded. Two guards held on every batch: the native plans put back in their own slots reproduce their features exactly (drift 0.0), and the scorer on the cache reproduces the model's own logits (drift 0.0). 1,536 sampled plans re-graded with the original grader all agreed. Each scene was then held out in turn and the scorer trained on the other four (20 epochs, constants fixed in advance, nothing tuned on the held-out scene).

Revised vocabulary (9 Oct, after review)

A second agent reviewed the first vocabulary over all 150 round-0 episodes (runs 0-2 to design, runs 3-9 held out) and found its main problem. Rescue ticks come after the released model has braked (median 3.1 m/s), and a car can only move sideways as fast as it moves forward. At that speed the first vocabulary's swerves need turning circles of 0.1 to 1.9 m: most of its "safe" paths cannot be driven. The revised set is drivable by construction, and it is what the cache is being built with.

32 pathsbuilt from
14the pick's path, driven at the current speed, moved over by ±1.0 to ±4.0 m in 0.5 m steps, measured across the path
14straight ahead at the current speed, the same 14 offsets
4the pick's path braking at 3 m/s², nudged ±1.0 and ±1.5 m

Each path moves over along a cosine curve and stays there. The curve advances with distance travelled (a car cannot crab sideways) and is as short as two limits allow: 4 m/s² sideways and a 5 m turning radius. Both sides are always offered, because the safe side flips between Singapore and Boston and nothing the model sees at drive time says which. "Pass and come back", braking-based swerves and the pick's own speed profile were cut.

What this means

Under 2 s from an oncoming car there is almost never a drivable escape, for any vocabulary. That is physics: the released model has already slowed down, so the swerve has to start early. The gain is uneven by scene: in my check 0064 went from 6 to 14 and 0075 from 9 to 22, while 0053 went from 5 to 7 and 0058 did not move. Coverage is measured; whether the scorer will pick these paths is not.

Why the trained scorers fail

On the held-out scene, the scorer's ranking loss ends at 3.45 (s1), 3.58 (s2) and 3.56 (s3). A scorer that ranks the 32 plans at random scores 3.47. Its pick is the best plan on 23%, 7% and 4% of held-out ticks, where chance is 3%. On the four training scenes it reaches 22 to 49%: it memorises them. Each step also reruns the 300 M-parameter image encoder, so a run gets only about 2,000 steps at batch 8.

The test this feeds

Run the frozen released model once per tick and save what its scorer reads. Then train on that cache, holding out one scene at a time, which takes minutes per run. This settles whether five scenes are enough to learn from at all, or whether earlier runs were just under-trained.

The target

Three yes/no answers per plan, graded against where every vehicle really went. No drawn expert is involved.

answerlabel
hitthe footprint overlaps any vehicle within 4 s, or within 1 s after that at its final speed; counts with the ego stopped too, as neuroNCAP does
off-roada footprint corner leaves the drivable map within 4 s
off-lanethe largest distance from the lane centre is over 1.75 m

A fixed formula picks the plan. Its constants are tuned on the training folds only:

score = (1 − Phit)4 × (1 − Poff-road) × (1 − 0.3·Poff-lane) × min(1, travel ÷ travel of the released pick)

On a normal road the released pick wins, because a swerve pays the off-lane cost for no gain. In a frontal scenario a swerve wins, because the straight plan hits. Faster plans gain nothing, which blocks the s1 to s3 habit of picking 17 to 22 m plans where 14 to 16 m was right. The current blended target trained the same way runs alongside as the control.

The first vocabulary

32 paths built every tick from the released model's own pick, so they follow the road's curve.

choicevalues
sideways offset−3.5, −2.5, −1.5, −1.0, +1.0, +1.5, +2.5, +3.5 m (left positive)
speedhold the current speed, or brake to 40% of it by 4 s (70% of the distance)
shapemove over in 2 s and stay; or move over in 1.5 s, hold for 1 s, come back by 4 s

How the paths get into the model

  1. Pass 1. The unchanged model runs: 32 native plans, their features, and the released pick.
  2. Build the 32 paths from the pick and the current speed.
  3. Pass 2 reruns only the refiner's last stage, with the same image features and queries. Each new path takes the slot of the native plan it is closest to (one-to-one), so its memory comes from a similar plan. The refiner samples the image along each new path. The 300 M encoder does not run again; this costs a few milliseconds.
  4. Score all 64 and let the formula pick.

It has to be a second pass because the refiner's query grid is fixed at 32 × 8. Guard: putting the native 32 back into their own slots in pass 2 must reproduce pass 1 bit for bit.

Which speed the swerves should use

The first version built the swerves on the pick's own speed. By the time the car is close, the released model has already slowed: at the three frontal ticks chosen by the rule, its pick covers 7.5 to 7.8 m in 4 s while the ego is doing 3.7 to 4.7 m/s. The second version keeps the pick's path but drives it at the current speed, which covers 15 to 19 m in 4 s.

What to check

  • Do the swerves look like moves a driver could make: 1 to 3.5 m over 2 s at the current speed?
  • On frontal ticks, is at least one path safe where the pick hits? The table above has the counts.
  • Scene 0070: the released model drifts 2 to 2.6 m off the human's path within 3.5 s in every shape, so its own plans are graded off-lane on every tick where they do not hit (60 of 70, 38 of 64 and 70 of 70 ticks). Is 1.75 m the right off-lane line?
  • Are the off-road verdicts right? They come from the nuScenes drivable map at 0.25 m. Some edges in the top-down views may be stricter than the real kerb.
  • frontal-0064: its car does not render, so it adds hit labels the image cannot support. It is in the data as you asked, and its fold will be reported on its own.
  • Paths never steer back after "move over and stay". That is fine for picking a plan every 0.5 s, but it is not a driving style.
